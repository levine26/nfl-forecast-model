# ATS Problem Reformulation

## 1. Why margin MAE and ATS are different objectives

A mean-margin forecast minimizes squared error when correctly specified; a median forecast minimizes absolute error. A spread wager, however, pays according to whether the realized integer margin falls above, exactly on, or below a quoted boundary. The relevant decision therefore depends on the conditional distribution near that boundary, not only on the conditional mean.

A forecast can improve MAE while moving probability mass across a key spread in the wrong way. Conversely, a distribution can have similar central-error metrics yet provide better-calibrated cover/push probabilities. This is the scientific reason for the new program; it is not permission to ignore the prior failure to beat the market in continuous margin error.

## 2. Canonical variables

Define:

- `M = home_score - away_score` (integer final margin).
- `L = sportsbook home spread`, where favorites carry negative spread.
- `S = -L`, the market's implied home margin.
- `R = M + L = M - S`, the market-relative realized residual.

Home ATS outcome:

- cover: `R > 0`;
- push: `R = 0`;
- loss: `R < 0`.

Examples:

| Quote | Final home margin | R | Home ATS result |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| home -3 | +4 | +1 | cover |
| home -3 | +3 | 0 | push |
| home -3 | +2 | -1 | loss |
| home +3 | -2 | +1 | cover |
| home +3 | -3 | 0 | push |
| home +3 | -4 | -1 | loss |

These rows are mandatory synthetic unit tests in Phase 2.

## 3. Quantile decision theory

Dmochowski (PLOS ONE, 2023, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0287601) formalizes spread wagering using outcome quantiles. Under the paper's continuous-margin approximation, a push has probability zero. If a unit-risk winning wager returns net `phi`, positive EV on one side requires a success probability greater than `1/(1+phi)`.

At standard -110, risking 110 to win 100 gives `phi=100/110=10/11`; the break-even win probability is:

`p_BE = 1/(1+10/11) = 11/21 = 0.5238095238`.

The complementary CDF probability is:

`1-p_BE = 10/21 = 0.4761904762`.

That yields the familiar lower/median/upper quantile targets `10/21`, `1/2`, and `11/21` under the continuous/no-push formulation.

The paper also empirically finds that the sportsbook spread strongly tracks the conditional median, reinforcing that the market is the correct null rather than a generic input feature.

## 4. Why `.476/.524` is not universal

NFL margins are discrete. Whole-number spreads can push, and actual quoted prices need not be -110.

For risk `R_bet` and net win `W_bet`:

`EV = P(win)*W_bet - P(loss)*R_bet`.

Pushes return the stake and contribute zero profit/loss.

For a negative American price `-a`:

`p_BE_decisive = a/(a+100)`.

For positive `+a`:

`p_BE_decisive = 100/(a+100)`.

If `q=P(push)`, then the decisive-outcome condition is:

`P(win)/(P(win)+P(loss)) > p_BE_decisive`,

or equivalently

`P(win) > (1-q)*p_BE_decisive`.

Thus standard `.476/.524` quantiles remain a useful frozen reference for Q1, while Q2/Q3 and wager EV must explicitly retain push probability and actual price where price exists.

## 5. Three statistical objects

The program intentionally separates:

### Conditional location

Where is the conditional median/nearby ATS-relevant quantile relative to the quoted market line? Q1 addresses this through quantile regression on `R`.

### Conditional scale/tails

How dispersed is the margin distribution around its center, and how do tails vary with spread/total state? Q2 addresses this with a bounded heavy-tail-capable base family and conditional scale.

### Discrete scoring mass

How much probability lies at integer key margins such as ±3, ±6, ±7, ±10 and ±14 (and tie margin 0)? Q2 models this explicitly rather than smearing it through a continuous bridge.

Q3 then asks whether direct cover/push/loss learning adds information not captured by the first two objects.

## 6. Market total interaction

Market total is admitted because total and spread jointly encode expected scoring environment and because margin variance/key-number relevance need not be constant across low- and high-total games.

V1 freezes one parsimonious interaction: `abs(S) * centered_total` in the Q2 conditional-scale model. Q1/Q3 may receive spread, absolute spread and total as main effects. Arbitrary polynomial/interactions are prohibited.

## 7. Personnel and contextual variables

QB status, injuries, OL continuity, explosiveness, weather and pace can plausibly affect conditional variance. But reconstructing those histories at a reliable PIT standard would turn this sprint into the separate Player-State program.

V1 therefore includes only existing, clearly pregame-shifted team-state variables already generated by the LevLine feature pipeline. No new historical personnel reconstruction is allowed. Weather/injury/QB-specific variance modeling is a later extension only after the core ATS formulation is evaluated.

## 8. Key numbers and era

American football's scoring increments create non-smooth margin distributions. Exact mass around 3 and 7 is well established and peer-reviewed work in 2026 explicitly treats 3/7 as payoff discontinuities. Practitioner/reproducible historical analyses also motivate inspection of 6, 10 and 14.

V1 does **not** import any external key-mass parameter. The preregistered key set is fixed before candidate outcomes, but all excess-mass values are learned only from prior eligible training history.

The 2015 regular season is the V1 floor because the NFL extra-point rule changed that year. Pre-2015 key-number frequencies are not silently pooled into the modern scoring regime.

## 9. Proper-scoring principle

The primary scientific question is probability quality. Therefore Q1 is selected by pinball loss; Q2 by discrete/distributional proper scores; Q3 and any Q2/Q3 blend by multinomial log loss. ATS hit rate and realized ROI are reported outcomes, never model-selection objectives.

## 10. What would falsify the program

The distributional reformulation is not presumed superior. It fails to establish incremental value if, under frozen chronology:

- Q1 does not improve relevant quantile loss over market-only calibration;
- Q2 does not improve distribution/push/cover calibration over M1/M2;
- Q3 does not improve direct probability scoring beyond Q2;
- any apparent selective ATS advantage is unstable across seasons, poorly calibrated or unsupported by uncertainty;
- improvements vanish against market-only calibrated nulls.

Such a null result is scientifically acceptable and closes the corresponding hypothesis.

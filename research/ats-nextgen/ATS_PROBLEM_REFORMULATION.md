# ATS Problem Reformulation

## 1. Why margin MAE and ATS are different estimands

A conditional-mean forecast minimizes squared error; a conditional median minimizes absolute error. Neither objective directly answers whether a team covers an exact quoted spread at an exact price.

For home margin `M`, sportsbook home spread `L`, and ATS residual `R=M+L`:

- `R>0`: home covers;
- `R=0`: push;
- `R<0`: home loses ATS.

The ATS problem is therefore about the conditional distribution of `R` around zero, not only `E[M|X]`.

A margin model can have lower MAE yet poor cover probabilities if its conditional variance, skew, tail shape or integer mass is wrong. Conversely, a model can be useful for probability calibration without materially improving mean-margin MAE.

## 2. Median and quantile decision theory

For pinball loss at quantile `τ`:

`ρτ(u) = u(τ - 1[u<0])`.

The conditional `τ` quantile minimizes expected pinball loss. The conditional median (`τ=0.5`) is therefore the natural location object for absolute-deviation decisions.

Dmochowski (2023, PLOS ONE) derives an optimal wagering framework in terms of the outcome distribution and sportsbook proposition. Under the paper's standard payout idealization `φ≈0.91`, the relevant no-push probability cutoffs correspond approximately to:

- lower quantile `1/(1+1/φ) = 10/21 ≈ 0.476190`;
- median `1/2`;
- upper quantile `1/(1+φ) = 11/21 ≈ 0.523810`.

Equivalently, at -110 with no push, break-even win probability is `110/(110+100)=11/21`; the complementary lower tail is `10/21`.

**Important:** these quantiles are not universal betting thresholds. Whole-number NFL spreads have nonzero push probability, and real quoted prices vary. Exact wagering decisions must use `P(win)`, `P(push)`, `P(loss)` and the actual payoff contract.

## 3. Exact price-aware decision rule

For risk `R$` to win `W$`:

`EV = P(win)*W$ - P(loss)*R$`.

Push returns stake and contributes zero profit/loss. A bet is positive expected value iff this quantity is positive.

At negative American odds `-a`, risk `a` to win 100; at positive odds `+a`, risk 100 to win `a`.

When push probability is zero, the familiar break-even probability is recovered. With pushes, the relevant condition is on the non-push win/loss masses, not simply `P(win)>0.5238`.

## 4. Discrete NFL margins

NFL final margins are integer-valued and generated by scoring events, so probability mass is not smooth. Q2 therefore treats margin as a PMF `P(M=k)` rather than a continuous Normal variable.

The preregistered key margins are:

`±3, ±6, ±7, ±10, ±14`.

These are fixed before candidate performance. Their excess mass and era dependence are estimated only from prior training history.

## 5. Three separate modeling components

The program deliberately separates:

1. **location/quantiles** — Q1 estimates market-relative conditional quantiles;
2. **dispersion and discrete mass** — Q2 estimates conditional scale, tails and key-number PMF;
3. **direct decision probability** — Q3 estimates cover/push/loss without requiring Q1/Q2's generative distribution assumptions.

This permits scientific ablation. If Q3 improves proper scores while Q2 does not, the distributional assumptions are suspect. If Q2 is calibrated and Q3 adds nothing, a direct classifier is unnecessary. If neither beats market nulls, the formulation is rejected rather than rescued.

## 6. Market total interaction

The market total is allowed to condition the margin distribution because game scoring environment can plausibly change variance and the relevance of fixed scoring increments.

V1 freezes only two structured uses:

- Q1 market terms include total plus `favorite_size × centered_total` and `signed_market_margin × centered_total`;
- Q2 conditional log-scale includes favorite size, centered total and their interaction.

No arbitrary interaction expansion is permitted.

## 7. Whole- and half-point grading

If `L` is half-integer, `M+L` cannot equal zero for integer `M`; `P(push)=0` by construction.

If `L` is integer, push probability is exactly the PMF mass at `M=-L`.

This rule is mandatory in both Q2 and Q3 and must be covered by synthetic tests before any historical candidate execution.
